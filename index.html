37,000 SKUs. Seven markets. One source of truth.

Midland Industries distributes and manufactures more than 37,000 SKUs of fittings, hose, valves and accessories for industrial, hose, fluid power, plumbing, oil and gas, truck and trailer, and power transmission customers, from ten facilities in the US and Canada. This page is a short look at how Salsify keeps a catalog that size consistent across every channel.

Why it matters now

Acquired catalogs, one customer-facing standard

A company built through acquisition inherits several part-numbering schemes, attribute conventions and catalog systems. Customers do not care which legacy company a fitting came from; they want thread size, material, pressure rating and a cross-reference, the same way every time. Salsify is where that standard gets built and enforced.

Dimensional attributes

Thread type, size, material, pressure and temperature ratings define every SKU and have to be validated, not typed.

Market-specific catalogs

The same fitting sells into plumbing, fluid power and truck and trailer with different terminology and cross-references.

Distributor and portal feeds

Customers and distributors pull Midland content into their own systems in their own formats.

The pattern we see

Where product content usually breaks down

We have not audited Midland's systems. What follows is the pattern we see at distributors and manufacturers built through acquisition and the questions we would like to check against yours.

Several legacy catalogs

  • Acquired companies brought their own part numbers, attributes and systems
  • Cross-references between them are maintained in spreadsheets
  • Engineering drawings, images and ERP item data live in separate places

Channel re-keying

  • Distributor portals, customer feeds and the website each get their own version of the same fitting
  • Spec updates reach the ERP first and the channels later
  • Nobody knows which of 37,000 listings is incomplete until a customer finds it
